%% prophets | history | Stories of the prophets | ނަބީބޭކަލުންގެ ވާހަކަތައް | The lives of the prophets as the Quran tells them, and what each teaches. | ޤުރުއާނުގައިވާ ގޮތަށް ނަބީބޭކަލުންގެ ޙަޔާތާއި، އެއިން ލިބޭ ޢިބްރަތްތައް.

@@ prophet-adam
title_en: Adam, the first human
title_dv: އާދަމްގެފާނު: ފުރަތަމަ އިންސާނާ
ref: Quran 2:30-39; 7:11-25; 20:115-123
--- en
Allah told the angels He would place a successor on earth, created Adam from clay, breathed spirit into him and taught him the names of all things. The angels were commanded to bow in honour of Adam. All did except **Iblis**, a jinn, who refused out of pride: "I am better than him. You created me from fire and him from clay."

Adam and his wife **Hawwa** lived in the Garden, free to eat from everything but one tree. Iblis whispered to them both, and they ate. The Quran does not blame Hawwa alone. They turned to Allah at once: "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will surely be among the losers" (7:23). Allah forgave them and sent them to earth with a promise: guidance will come, and whoever follows it has nothing to fear.

## What it teaches
- Every person is born free of sin; there is no inherited guilt.
- Pride is the first sin, and repentance is the first answer to sin.
- Satan is an open enemy whose only power is to whisper.
- People are one family from one father and mother.
--- dv
ބިމުގައި ޚަލީފާއަކު ލައްވާނެކަމަށް ﷲ މަލާއިކަތުންނަށް ވަޙީކުރައްވައި، މަށިން އާދަމްގެފާނު ހައްދަވައި، ރޫޙު ފުމެލައްވައި، ހުރިހާ އެއްޗެއްގެ ނަންތައް ދަސްކޮށްދެއްވިއެވެ. އާދަމްގެފާނަށް ސަޖިދަކުރުމަށް މަލާއިކަތުންނަށް އަމުރުކުރެއްވުމުން، ޖިންނިއެއް ކަމުގައިވާ **އިބްލީސް** ފިޔަވައި އެންމެން ސަޖިދަކުރިއެވެ. އޭނާ ދެކޮޅުހެދީ ކިބުރުވެރިކަމުންނެވެ.

އާދަމްގެފާނާއި **ޙައްވާގެފާނު** ސުވަރުގޭގައި ދިރިއުޅުއްވިއެވެ. އެއް ގަހެއް ފިޔަވައި ހުރިހާ އެއްޗަކުން ކެއުން ހުއްދައެވެ. އިބްލީސް ދެބޭކަލުންނަށްވެސް ވަސްވާސްދިނުމުން އެ ގަހުން ފަރީއްކުޅުއްވިއެވެ. ވަގުތުން ތައުބާވެ ދުޢާކުރެއްވިއެވެ: "އަޅަމެންގެ ވެރިރަސްކަލާނގެއެވެ! އަޅަމެން އަޅަމެންގެ ނަފްސުތަކަށް އަނިޔާވެރިވެއްޖައީމުއެވެ" (7:23). ﷲ ފުއްސަވައި، ބިމަށް ފޮނުއްވިއެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ކޮންމެ އިންސާނަކު އުފަންވަނީ ފާފައެއް ނެތިއެވެ.
- ފުރަތަމަ ފާފައަކީ ކިބުރެވެ. ފާފައިގެ ފުރަތަމަ ޖަވާބަކީ ތައުބާއެވެ.
- ޝައިޠާނާއަކީ ފާޅު ޢަދުއްވެކެވެ. އޭނާއަށް ކުރެވެނީ ވަސްވާސްދިނުން އެކަންޏެވެ.
- އިންސާނުންނަކީ އެއް މަންމައާއި ބައްޕައެއްގެ އެއް ޢާއިލާއެކެވެ.

@@ prophet-nuh
title_en: Nuh and the flood
title_dv: ނޫޙުގެފާނާއި ތޫފާން
ref: Quran 11:25-49; 71; 23:23-30
--- en
Generations after Adam, people began to honour images of righteous men and then to worship them. Allah sent **Nuh**, the first messenger to a people who had fallen into idolatry.

He called them for **950 years** (29:14), by night and by day, in public and in private. The leaders mocked him and said only the lowly followed him. Few believed.

Allah then told him that no more would believe, and commanded him to build a ship. His people laughed at a ship being built on dry land. When the water came from the sky and the earth, he boarded the believers and a pair of every creature. His own son refused, saying he would climb a mountain, and was drowned. Nuh's wife also disbelieved. The ship came to rest on Mount Judi.

When Nuh pleaded for his son, Allah answered: "He is not of your family; his conduct was unrighteous" (11:46).

## What it teaches
- A caller to Allah needs long patience and is not judged by numbers.
- Faith, not blood, is what ties people before Allah.
- Take the means Allah commands, even when others cannot see the sense in them.
--- dv
އާދަމްގެފާނަށްފަހު ޖީލުތަކެއް ވުމުން، މީސްތަކުން ޞާލިޙު މީހުންގެ ސޫރަތަކަށް އިޙްތިރާމުކުރަން ފަށައި، ފަހުން އެ ސޫރަތަކަށް އަޅުކަންކުރަން ފެށިއެވެ. ބުދު އަޅުކަމަށް ވެއްޓުނު ޤައުމަކަށް ﷲ ފޮނުއްވި ފުރަތަމަ ރަސޫލަކީ **ނޫޙުގެފާނެވެ**.

**950 އަހަރު** (29:14) ރެއާއި ދުވާލު، ފާޅުގައާއި ސިއްރުގައި ދަޢުވަތު ދެއްވިއެވެ. ވެރިން މަލާމާތްކުރިއެވެ. އީމާންވީ މަދު ބަޔެކެވެ.

އެއަށްފަހު ނަވެއް ބަނުމަށް ﷲ އަމުރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ. ހިކި ބިމުގައި ނަވެއް ބަންނާތީ ޤައުމުގެ މީހުން ހޭންފެށިއެވެ. އުޑުންނާއި ބިމުން ފެން އައުމުން، މުއުމިނުންނާއި ކޮންމެ ދިރުމަކުން ޖޯޑެއް ނަވަށް އެރުވިއެވެ. އެކަލޭގެފާނުގެ ދަރިކަލުން ދެކޮޅުހަދައި ގެނބުނެވެ. ނަވު ހުއްޓުނީ ޖޫދީ ފަރުބަދަ މަތީގައެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ދަޢުވަތުދޭ މީހާއަށް ދިގު ކެތްތެރިކަމެއް ބޭނުންވެއެވެ. ކާމިޔާބީ މިންކުރެވެނީ ޢަދަދަކުން ނޫނެވެ.
- ﷲ ގެ ޙަޟްރަތުގައި މީހުން ގުޅުވައިދެނީ އީމާންކަމެވެ. ލޭގެ ގުޅުމެއް ނޫނެވެ.

@@ prophet-ibrahim
title_en: Ibrahim, the friend of Allah
title_dv: އިބްރާހީމްގެފާނު: ﷲ ގެ ޚަލީލު
ref: Quran 6:74-83; 21:51-70; 37:99-111; 2:124-129; 14:35-41
--- en
**Ibrahim** grew up among idol-makers; his own father carved them. As a young man he reasoned with his people: a star, the moon and the sun all set, and what sets cannot be Lord. One day he broke their idols and left the largest standing. "Ask it," he said. They threw him into a fire, and Allah commanded: "O fire, be coolness and safety for Ibrahim" (21:69).

He left his homeland for Allah's sake. In old age he was given two sons, **Isma'il** and **Ishaq**. He settled Hajar and the infant Isma'il in the barren valley of Makkah by Allah's command; her search for water between Safa and Marwah is repeated by every pilgrim, and the spring of Zamzam flows there still.

He saw in a dream that he was to sacrifice his son. Both submitted, and Allah ransomed the boy with a ram. This is remembered each year at Eid al-Adha.

With Isma'il he raised the **Ka'bah** and prayed: "Our Lord, accept this from us" (2:127).

## What it teaches
- Use reason to arrive at truth, then hold to it whatever the cost.
- Complete trust: he left what he loved most when commanded.
- He is called *Khalilullah*, the intimate friend of Allah, and Muslims follow his way.
--- dv
**އިބްރާހީމްގެފާނު** ބޮޑުވެވަޑައިގަތީ ބުދު ހަދާ ބައެއްގެ ތެރޭގައެވެ. ޒުވާން ދުވަސްވަރު ޤައުމުގެ މީހުންނާ ބަހުސްކުރެއްވިއެވެ: ތަރިއާއި، ހަނދާއި، އިރު އޮއްސޭ ހިނދު، އޮއްސޭ އެއްޗެއް އިލާހަކަށް ނުވާނެއެވެ. އެއް ދުވަހަކު އެމީހުންގެ ބުދުތައް ތަޅުއްވާލައްވައި، އެންމެ ބޮޑު ބުދު ދޫކޮށްލެއްވިއެވެ. އެމީހުން އެކަލޭގެފާނު އަލިފާނަށް އެއްލުމުން ﷲ އަމުރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ: "އޭ އަލިފާނާއެވެ! އިބްރާހީމްގެފާނަށް ފިނިކަމަކާއި ސަލާމަތްކަމެއް ކަމުގައި ވާށެވެ!" (21:69).

ﷲ އަށްޓަކައި ޤައުމު ދޫކުރެއްވިއެވެ. މުސްކުޅި ޢުމުރުގައި **އިސްމާޢީލާއި** **އިސްޙާޤު** ދެއްވިއެވެ. ﷲ ގެ އަމުރުފުޅަށް ހާޖަރުގެފާނާއި ތުއްތު އިސްމާޢީލް މައްކާގެ ހިކި ވާދީގައި ބެހެއްޓެވިއެވެ. ޞަފާއާއި މަރްވާއާ ދެމެދު ފެން ހޯއްދެވުން ކޮންމެ ޙައްޖުވެރިއަކު ތަކުރާރުކުރެއެވެ.

ދަރިކަލުން ޤުރުބާންކުރުމަށް ހުވަފެނުން ދެކެވަޑައިގަތެވެ. ދެބޭކަލުންވެސް ކިޔަމަންވުމުން، ﷲ ކަންބަޅިއެއް ފިދާއަކަށް ދެއްވިއެވެ. ކޮންމެ އަހަރަކު އަޟްޙާ ޢީދުގައި މިކަން ހަނދާންކުރެވެއެވެ.

އިސްމާޢީލްގެފާނާއެކު **ކަޢުބާ** ބިނާކުރެއްވިއެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ބުއްދި ބޭނުންކޮށްގެން ޙައްޤު ހޯދައި، އެއަށްފަހު ކޮންމެ އަގެއް ދީގެންވެސް އެ ޙައްޤުގައި ހިފެހެއްޓުން.
- ފުރިހަމަ ތަވައްކުލު.
- އެކަލޭގެފާނަށް *ޚަލީލުﷲ* ގެ ލަޤަބު ދެއްވިއެވެ.

@@ prophet-yusuf
title_en: Yusuf, from the well to the treasury
title_dv: ޔޫސުފުގެފާނު: ވަޅުން ޚަޒާނާއަށް
ref: Quran 12
--- en
Surah Yusuf tells one story from start to finish and calls it "the best of stories".

As a boy **Yusuf** dreamt that eleven stars, the sun and the moon bowed to him. His father **Ya'qub** warned him not to tell his brothers. Jealous of their father's love for him, they threw him into a well and brought home his shirt stained with false blood. Ya'qub answered with "beautiful patience".

A caravan sold Yusuf in Egypt to a nobleman. When he grew up, the nobleman's wife tried to seduce him. He said, "I seek refuge in Allah", and ran. Though his innocence was proven, he was imprisoned. In prison he called his companions to the one God and interpreted their dreams.

Years later the king dreamt of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones. Yusuf explained it as seven years of plenty and seven of famine, was cleared and put in charge of the storehouses. His brothers came for grain and did not recognise him. In the end he said: "No blame on you today. May Allah forgive you" (12:92). His parents and brothers came to Egypt, and the dream came true.

## What it teaches
- Allah's plan unfolds over years; hardship may be the road to honour.
- Chastity is possible under the strongest pressure.
- Forgive when you have the power to punish.
--- dv
ޔޫސުފު ސޫރަތުގައި ފެށުނީއްސުރެ ނިމެންދެން އެއް ވާހަކައެއް ކިޔައިދެއެވެ. އެއަށް "އެންމެ ރިވެތި ވާހަކަ" ކަމަށް ވެއެވެ.

ކުޑައިރު **ޔޫސުފުގެފާނު** ހުވަފެނުން ދެކެވަޑައިގަތީ އެގާރަ ތަރިއާއި، އިރާއި، ހަނދު އެކަލޭގެފާނަށް ސަޖިދަކުރާ ތަނެވެ. ބައްޕާފުޅު **ޔަޢުޤޫބުގެފާނުގެ** ލޯބި އެކަލޭގެފާނަށް ލިބޭތީ ޙަސަދަވެރިވި ބޭބެމެން، އެކަލޭގެފާނު ވަޅަކަށް އެއްލިއެވެ. ޔަޢުޤޫބުގެފާނު "ރިވެތި ކެތްތެރިކަން" އިޚްތިޔާރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ.

ޤާފިލާއަކުން އެކަލޭގެފާނު މިޞްރުގައި ވިއްކާލިއެވެ. ބޮޑުވުމުން ޢަޒީޒުގެ އަނބިކަނބަލުން ފާޙިޝް ޢަމަލަށް ގޮވާލިއެވެ. "ﷲ ގެ ރައްކާތެރިކަން އެދެމެވެ" ވިދާޅުވެ ދުރުވެވަޑައިގަތެވެ. ކުށެއް ނެތްކަން ސާބިތުވިނަމަވެސް ޖަލަށް ލެވުނެވެ. ޖަލުގައިވެސް ތައުޙީދަށް ދަޢުވަތު ދެއްވިއެވެ.

އަހަރުތަކެއްފަހުން ރަސްގެފާނުގެ ހުވަފެން ތަޢުބީރުކޮށްދެއްވުމުން ޚަޒާނާތަކާ ޙަވާލުކުރެވުނެވެ. ބޭބެމެން ގޮވާން ހޯދަން އައުމުން ވިދާޅުވިއެވެ: "މިއަދު ތިޔަބައިމީހުންގެ މައްޗަށް އެއްވެސް މަލާމާތެއް ނެތެވެ. ﷲ ތިޔަބައިމީހުންނަށް ފުއްސަވާށިއެވެ" (12:92).

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ﷲ ގެ ރޭވުން ފުރިހަމަވަނީ އަހަރުތަކެއްގެ ތެރޭގައެވެ. ދަތިކަމަކީ ޝަރަފުގެ މަގު ކަމުގައި ވެދާނެއެވެ.
- އެންމެ ބޮޑު ފިއްތުމުގައިވެސް ޢިއްފަތްތެރިވެވިދާނެއެވެ.
- ބަދަލު ހިފޭނެ ބާރު އޮތްއިރު މާފުކުރުން.

title_en: Musa and Pharaoh
title_dv: މޫސާގެފާނާއި ފިރުޢައުނު
ref: Quran 28:1-43; 20:9-98; 26:10-68; 7:103-156
--- en
**Musa** is the prophet mentioned most often in the Quran.

Pharaoh enslaved the Children of Israel and killed their newborn sons. Musa's mother was inspired to set her baby afloat on the river. Pharaoh's own household picked him up, and his wife **Asiyah** persuaded Pharaoh to keep him. He grew up in the palace.

As a young man he struck an Egyptian who was beating an Israelite, and the man died. Musa fled to Madyan, helped two women water their flock, married one and worked ten years as a shepherd. Returning, he saw a fire on Mount Sinai. There Allah spoke to him, gave him the signs of the staff and the shining hand, and sent him with his brother **Harun** to Pharaoh: "Speak to him gently" (20:44).

Pharaoh called it magic and gathered his magicians. Their ropes seemed to move; Musa's staff swallowed them, and the magicians fell in prostration and believed. Plagues followed, yet Pharaoh persisted. Musa led his people out by night. At the sea he said, "My Lord is with me; He will guide me" (26:62). The sea parted, and Pharaoh drowned.

In the desert Musa received the **Tawrah**. While he was away his people worshipped a golden calf, and they wandered forty years for refusing to enter the holy land.

## What it teaches
- Allah's help reaches the oppressed through means no one expects.
- Speak gently even to a tyrant.
- Being freed is easier than staying grateful.

ޤުރުއާނުގައި އެންމެ ގިނައިން ނަންފުޅު އައިސްފައިވާ ނަބިއްޔަކީ **މޫސާގެފާނެވެ**.

ފިރުޢައުނު ބަނީ އިސްރާއީލުން އަޅުވެތިކޮށް، އެމީހުންގެ ފިރިހެން ތުއްތު ކުދިން މަރާލިއެވެ. މޫސާގެފާނުގެ މަންމާފުޅަށް އިލްހާމްކުރެވުނީ ދަރިކަލުން ކޯރަށް ދޫކޮށްލެއްވުމަށެވެ. ފިރުޢައުނުގެ ގަނޑުވަރުގެ މީހުން އެކަލޭގެފާނު ނެގިއެވެ. އޭނާގެ އަނބިކަނބަލުން **އާސިޔާގެ** އެދިވަޑައިގަތުމަށް ގަނޑުވަރުގައި ބޮޑުކުރެވުނެވެ.

ޒުވާން ދުވަސްވަރު މަދްޔަނަށް ހިޖުރަކުރައްވައި، ކައިވެނިކުރައްވައި، ދިހަ އަހަރު ބަކަރި ހުއިހެއްޕެވިއެވެ. އެނބުރި ވަޑައިގަންނަވަމުން ޠޫރުސީނާ ފަރުބަދައިގައި ﷲ އެކަލޭގެފާނާ ވާހަކަފުޅު ދެއްކެވިއެވެ. އަސާކޮޅާއި އަލިވާ އަތްޕުޅުގެ މުޢުޖިޒާތް ދެއްވައި، ބޭބޭފުޅު **ހާރޫނުގެފާނާއެކު** ފިރުޢައުނުގެ ގާތަށް ފޮނުއްވިއެވެ: "އޭނާއާ މަޑުމައިތިރި ބަހުން ވާހަކަދައްކާށެވެ!" (20:44).

ފިރުޢައުނު ސިޙުރުވެރިން އެއްކުރިއެވެ. މޫސާގެފާނުގެ އަސާކޮޅު އެމީހުންގެ ސިޙުރު ދިރުވާލުމުން، ސިޙުރުވެރިން ސަޖިދަކޮށް އީމާންވިއެވެ. މޫސާގެފާނު ރޭގަނޑު ޤައުމުގެ މީހުންނާއެކު ނުކުމެވަޑައިގަތެވެ. ކަނޑު ދެފަޅިވެ، ފިރުޢައުނު ގެނބުނެވެ.

ފަޅުތެރޭގައި **ތައުރާތު** ލިބިވަޑައިގަތެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- އަނިޔާ ލިބޭ މީހުންނަށް ﷲ ގެ ނަޞްރު އަންނަނީ ހީވެސް ނުކުރާ ގޮތްގޮތުންނެވެ.
- އަނިޔާވެރިޔަކާވެސް މަޑުމައިތިރިކޮށް ވާހަކަދެއްކުން.
- މިނިވަންވުމަށްވުރެ ޝުކުރުވެރިކަން ދެމެހެއްޓުން އުނދަގޫވެއެވެ.

## ދާއޫދުގެފާނު
ޠާލޫތުގެ ލަޝްކަރުގައި ހުންނެވި ޒުވާނަކަށް ހުންނަވައި **ދާއޫދުގެފާނު** ޖާލޫތު ޤަތުލުކުރެއްވިއެވެ. ﷲ އެކަލޭގެފާނަށް ރަސްކަމާއި ޙިކްމަތް ދެއްވިއެވެ (2:251). **ޒަބޫރު** ދެއްވިއެވެ. ފަރުބަދަތަކާއި ދޫނިތައް އެކަލޭގެފާނާއެކު ތަސްބީޙަކިޔައެވެ. ރަސްގެފާނަކަށް ހުންނަވައިވެސް އަމިއްލަ އަތްޕުޅުގެ މަސައްކަތުން ފަރީއްކުޅުއްވިއެވެ. އެއް ދުވަސް ދޫކޮށް އެއް ދުވަހު ރޯދަހިއްޕަވައި، ރޭގަނޑުގެ ތިންބައިކުޅަ އެއްބައި ނަމާދުކުރެއްވިއެވެ (ބުޚާރީ 1131).

## ސުލައިމާނުގެފާނު
ދަރިކަލުން **ސުލައިމާނުގެފާނަށް** ނަބީކަމާއި ރަސްކަން ލިބިވަޑައިގަތެވެ. ވައްޔާއި، ޖިންނީންނާއި، ދޫނިތައް އެކަލޭގެފާނަށް ކިޔަމަންކޮށްދެއްވިއެވެ. ހިނިތަކުގެ ވާހަކަ އަޑުއައްސަވައި، ހިނިތުންވެވަޑައިގެން ﷲ އަށް ޝުކުރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ (27:19).

ހުދުހުދު ދޫނި **ސަބައުގެ ރާނީގެ** ޚަބަރު ގެނައެވެ. އެ ރާނީގެ ޤައުމުގެ މީހުން އަޅުކަންކުރީ އިރަށެވެ. ސުލައިމާނުގެފާނު ސިޓީއެއް ފޮނުއްވިއެވެ. ރާނީ އައިސް އިސްލާމްވިއެވެ (27:44).

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ބާރާއި މުއްސަނދިކަން، ޢަދުލުވެރިކަމާއި ޝުކުރާއެކު ގެންގުޅެވިދާނެއެވެ.
- "މިއީ އަހުރެންގެ ވެރިރަސްކަލާނގެ ފަޟްލުފުޅެވެ. އަހުރެން ޝުކުރުކުރޭތޯ އިމްތިޙާނުކުރެއްވުމަށެވެ" (27:40).

## Ayyub
**Ayyub** had wealth, children and health, and was grateful. Then all three were taken. He was ill for years, and only his wife stayed by him. He never complained against Allah. His prayer did not even ask directly: "Harm has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful" (21:83). Allah told him to strike the ground with his foot; a spring rose, he washed and drank, and was healed. His family was restored and doubled. "We found him patient. What an excellent servant" (38:44).

## Yunus
**Yunus** was sent to Nineveh. When his people rejected him, he left in anger before Allah gave him leave, and boarded a ship. In a storm lots were drawn and he was cast overboard. A great fish swallowed him. In three darknesses, of the night, the sea and the fish, he called: "There is no god but You, glory be to You; I was among the wrongdoers" (21:87). Allah brought him out onto the shore and grew a plant to shade him. He returned to find that his people, more than a hundred thousand, had believed.

The Prophet ﷺ said no Muslim calls with this dua about anything without Allah answering him (Tirmidhi 3505).

## What they teach
- Patience is not silence about pain; it is not blaming Allah.
- Even a prophet returns to Allah after a slip, and is honoured for returning.
- No place is too dark for a dua to be heard.

## އައްޔޫބުގެފާނު
**އައްޔޫބުގެފާނަށް** މުދަލާއި، ދަރިންނާއި، ދުޅަހެޔޮކަން ލިބިވަޑައިގެންނެވިއެވެ. އަދި ޝުކުރުވެރި ބޭކަލެކެވެ. އެއަށްފަހު އެ ތިން ނިޢުމަތްވެސް ނަންގަވައިފިއެވެ. އަހަރުތަކެއް ވަންދެން ބަލިވެ އޮންނެވިއެވެ. ގާތުގައި ހުންނެވީ ހަމައެކަނި އަނބިކަނބަލުންނެވެ. ދުވަހަކުވެސް ﷲ އާމެދު ޝަކުވާއެއް ނުކުރައްވައެވެ. ދުޢާކުރެއްވީ: "އަހުރެންނަށް ގެއްލުން ޖެހިއްޖެއެވެ. އިބަރަސްކަލާނގެއީ ރަޙްމަތްތެރިންގެ ތެރެއިން އެންމެ ރަޙްމަތްވަންތަ ރަސްކަލާނގެއެވެ" (21:83). ﷲ ޝިފާ ދެއްވައި، ޢާއިލާ ދެ ގުނައަށް އަނބުރާ ދެއްވިއެވެ.

## ޔޫނުސްގެފާނު
**ޔޫނުސްގެފާނު** ފޮނުއްވުނީ ނީނަވާއަށެވެ. ޤައުމުގެ މީހުން ދެކޮޅުހެދުމުން، ﷲ ގެ ހުއްދައެއް ނެތި ކޯފާވެ ވަޑައިގެން ނަވަކަށް އެރިވަޑައިގަތެވެ. ތޫފާނެއްގައި ގުރުއަތުން ކަނޑަށް އެއްލެވުނެވެ. ބޮޑު މަހެއް ދިރުވާލިއެވެ. ތިން އަނދިރީގެ ތެރޭގައި ދުޢާކުރެއްވިއެވެ: "އިބަރަސްކަލާނގެ ފިޔަވައި އެހެން އިލާހަކު ނުވެއެވެ. އިބަރަސްކަލާނގެ ހުސްޠާހިރުވަންތައެވެ. ހަމަކަށަވަރުން އަހުރެންވީ އަނިޔާވެރިންގެ ތެރެއިންނެވެ" (21:87). ﷲ ސަލާމަތްކުރެއްވިއެވެ. އެނބުރި ވަޑައިގަތްއިރު ލައްކައަށްވުރެ ގިނަ މީހުން އީމާންވެފައިވެއެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- ކެތްތެރިކަމަކީ ﷲ އާމެދު ޝަކުވާނުކުރުމެވެ.
- ދުޢާ ނުއިވޭހާ އަނދިރި ތަނެއް ނުވެއެވެ.

## Maryam
The mother of **Maryam** dedicated her unborn child to Allah's service. Maryam grew up in the sanctuary under the care of the prophet **Zakariyya**, who would find provision with her out of season: "It is from Allah" (3:37). She is the only woman named in the Quran, and a surah bears her name.

An angel brought her news of a son without a father. "How can I have a son when no man has touched me?" "So it will be. Your Lord says: it is easy for Me" (19:20-21). She gave birth alone beneath a palm tree, and returned to her people carrying the child. When they accused her, she pointed to him, and the infant spoke: "I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet" (19:30).

## 'Isa
**'Isa** was sent to the Children of Israel with the **Injil**, confirming the Tawrah. By Allah's permission he healed the blind and the leper and raised the dead. His likeness before Allah is that of Adam: He created him from dust and said "Be" (3:59).

His enemies plotted to kill him. "They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them. Allah raised him up to Himself" (4:157-158). He will return before the Last Day.

## What Muslims believe about him
He is a servant and messenger of Allah, born of a virgin, a word from Allah and a spirit from Him. He is not divine and not the son of God. On the Day of Judgement he will say: "I told them only what You commanded me: worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord" (5:117).

## މަރްޔަމްގެފާނު
**މަރްޔަމްގެފާނުގެ** މަންމާފުޅު، ބަނޑުފުޅުގައިވާ ދަރިފުޅު ﷲ ގެ ޚިދުމަތަށް ނަދުރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ. މަރްޔަމްގެފާނު ބޮޑުވެވަޑައިގަތީ **ޒަކަރިއްޔާގެފާނުގެ** ބެލުމުގެ ދަށުން މިޙްރާބުގައެވެ. ޤުރުއާނުގައި ނަން އައިސްފައިވާ ހަމައެކަނި އަންހެން ބޭކަނބަލަކީ އެކަމަނާއެވެ.

ބައްޕައަކާ ނުލައި ދަރިކަލަކު ލިބިވަޑައިގަންނަވާނެކަމުގެ ޚަބަރު މަލާއިކަތަކު ގެނެސްދެއްވިއެވެ. "ތިބާގެ ވެރިރަސްކަލާނގެ ވަޙީކުރައްވައެވެ: އެއީ ތިމަންރަސްކަލާނގެއަށް ފަސޭހަ ކަމެކެވެ" (19:21). ކަދުރު ގަހެއްގެ ދަށުގައި އެކަނި ވިހެއުމަށްފަހު، ދަރިކަލުން ގޮވައިގެން ޤައުމުގެ މީހުންގެ ގާތަށް ވަޑައިގަތެވެ. އެމީހުން ތުހުމަތުކުރުމުން، ތުއްތު ދަރިކަލުން ވާހަކަފުޅު ދެއްކެވިއެވެ: "ހަމަކަށަވަރުން އަހުރެންނަކީ ﷲ ގެ އަޅާއެވެ" (19:30).

## ޢީސާގެފާނު
**ޢީސާގެފާނު** ބަނީ އިސްރާއީލުންނަށް **އިންޖީލާއެކު** ފޮނުއްވުނެވެ. ﷲ ގެ ހުއްދަފުޅާއެކު ކަނު މީހުންނާއި ޖުޒާމު ބަލި ހުރި މީހުންނަށް ޝިފާ ދެއްވައި، މަރުވެފައިވާ މީހުން ދިރުއްވިއެވެ.

"އެމީހުން އެކަލޭގެފާނު ޤަތުލެއް ނުކުރެއެވެ. އަދި ޞަލީބަކަށް ނުވެސް އަރުވައެވެ. ﷲ އެކަލޭގެފާނު އެކަލާނގެ ޙަޟްރަތަށް އުފުއްލެވިއެވެ" (4:157-158). ޤިޔާމަތުގެ ކުރިން އަލުން ފައިބާވަޑައިގަންނަވާނެއެވެ.

## މުސްލިމުންގެ ޢަޤީދާ
އެކަލޭގެފާނަކީ ﷲ ގެ އަޅެއް އަދި ރަސޫލެކެވެ. އިލާހެއް ނޫނެވެ. ﷲ ގެ ދަރިއެއްވެސް ނޫނެވެ.

The Quran often tells these four stories together. Each prophet said the same first words: "O my people, worship Allah; you have no god but Him." Each people had its own sin as well.

## Hud and 'Ad
'Ad were strong builders in southern Arabia, proud of their power: "Who is mightier than us?" They rejected **Hud**, and a furious wind destroyed them over seven nights and eight days.

## Salih and Thamud
Thamud carved homes into the rock. They asked **Salih** for a sign and were given a she-camel, with the command to let her drink and graze. They killed her, and a blast overtook them.

## Lut
**Lut**, the nephew of Ibrahim, was sent to cities whose men practised immorality openly and robbed travellers. They threatened to expel him for being "pure". The cities were overturned and rained with stones. His wife was among those who stayed behind.

## Shu'ayb and Madyan
The people of Madyan cheated in trade. **Shu'ayb** said: "Give full measure and weight, and do not deprive people of their due" (7:85). They asked whether his prayer told him to interfere with their money. An earthquake took them.

## What they teach
- Faith and honest dealing are not separate; cheating in the market drew a punishment like idolatry did.
- Strength, skill and wealth do not protect a people who are unjust.
- The ruins of earlier nations are left as signs: "Travel through the land and see how the deniers ended" (3:137).

ޤުރުއާނުގައި މި ހަތަރު ވާހަކަ ގިނައިން އެކުގައި ކިޔައިދެއެވެ. ކޮންމެ ނަބިއްޔަކުވެސް ފުރަތަމަ ވިދާޅުވީ އެއް ބަހެކެވެ: "އޭ އަހުރެންގެ ޤައުމުގެ މީހުންނޭވެ! ﷲ އަށް އަޅުކަންކުރާށެވެ!"

## ހޫދުގެފާނާއި ޢާދު ބާގައިގެ މީހުން
ޢާދު ބާގައިގެ މީހުންނަކީ އެމީހުންގެ ބާރާމެދު ފަޚުރުވެރިވި، ގަދަފަދަ ބައެކެވެ. **ހޫދުގެފާނަށް** ދެކޮޅުހެދުމުން، ހަތް ރެއާއި އަށް ދުވާލު ވަންދެން ކުރި ގަދަ ވަޔަކުން ހަލާކުކުރެވުނެވެ.

## ޞާލިޙުގެފާނާއި ޘަމޫދު ބާގައިގެ މީހުން
ޘަމޫދު ބާގައިގެ މީހުން ހިލަ ފަރުބަދަ ކޮނެގެން ގެދޮރު ހެދިއެވެ. **ޞާލިޙުގެފާނުގެ** ކިބައިން މުޢުޖިޒާތެއް އެދުމުން އަންހެން ޖަމަލެއް ދެއްވިއެވެ. އެމީހުން އެ ޖަމަލު މަރާލުމުން ބާރުގަދަ އަޑަކުން ހަލާކުކުރެވުނެވެ.

## ލޫޠުގެފާނު
**ލޫޠުގެފާނު** ފޮނުއްވުނީ ފާޙިޝް ޢަމަލު ފާޅުގައި ކުރާ، ދަތުރުވެރިން ފޭރޭ ބައެއްގެ ގާތަށެވެ. އެ ރަށްތައް ފުރޮޅާލައްވައި، ހިލައިގެ ވާރޭ ވެއްސެވިއެވެ.

## ޝުޢައިބުގެފާނާއި މަދްޔަން
މަދްޔަނުގެ މީހުން ވިޔަފާރީގައި އޮޅުވާލިއެވެ. **ޝުޢައިބުގެފާނު** ވިދާޅުވިއެވެ: "މިނުމާއި ކިރުން ފުރިހަމަކުރާށެވެ! އަދި މީސްތަކުންގެ ޙައްޤުތައް އުނިނުކުރާށެވެ!" (7:85).

## ޢިބްރަތްތައް
- އީމާންކަމާއި ތެދުވެރި މުޢާމަލާތަކީ ވަކިކުރެވޭނެ ދެ ކަމެއް ނޫނެވެ.
- ބާރާއި މުއްސަނދިކަން، އަނިޔާވެރި ޤައުމެއް ސަލާމަތެއް ނުކުރާނެއެވެ.

--- en
"Do not consume one another's wealth wrongfully, but trade by mutual consent" (4:29).

## What makes a sale valid
1. Both sides agree freely.
2. The seller owns the item or acts for the owner.
3. The item is lawful, known and can be handed over.
4. The price is known.

In the Shafi'i school the offer and acceptance are spoken, though many later scholars accept customary sales, such as taking an item and paying at a counter.

## The right to cancel
- **While still together:** either side may cancel before they part. "The two parties have the choice as long as they have not separated" (Bukhari 2079).
- **By condition:** they may agree a return period, up to three days.
- **For a defect:** a buyer who finds a hidden fault may return the item.

## Forbidden practices
- **Cheating and hiding defects.** The Prophet ﷺ found damp grain hidden under dry and said: "Whoever cheats us is not one of us" (Muslim 102).
- **Short measure or weight** (83:1-3).
- **Fake bidding** to push up a price.
- **Undercutting** a sale your brother has already agreed.
- **Hoarding** necessities to raise prices.
- **Selling what you do not have**, and sales with major uncertainty.
- **Riba**, covered in its own article.

## Good practice
Write down debts and have them witnessed (2:282). Be easy when buying, selling and collecting: "May Allah have mercy on a man who is generous when he sells, buys and asks for his due" (Bukhari 2076). Give more time to a debtor in difficulty (2:280).

The shares of inheritance are set by Allah in the Quran itself. They are not left to custom or to the wishes of the deceased.

## The order of dealing with an estate
1. Funeral costs.
2. Debts, including unpaid zakat.
3. Bequests, up to **one third**, and only to people who are not heirs. "A third, and a third is a lot" (Bukhari 2742).
4. The heirs' shares.

## The main fixed shares
- **Husband:** half if the wife left no child; a quarter if she did.
- **Wife:** a quarter if the husband left no child; an eighth if he did.
- **Mother:** a third; a sixth if there is a child or two or more siblings.
- **Father:** a sixth if there is a son; otherwise a sixth plus what remains.
- **Daughter with no son:** half if one; two thirds shared if two or more.
- **Sons and daughters together** take what remains, each son receiving the share of two daughters (4:11).

The son's larger share goes with his duty to pay the dowry and maintain his wife, children and, if needed, his parents and sisters. A woman's wealth is entirely her own.

## A worked example
A man dies leaving a wife, his mother, one son and one daughter. The estate after debts is MVR 720,000.
- Wife, one eighth: 90,000.
- Mother, one sixth: 120,000.
- Remainder 510,000 in three parts: son 340,000, daughter 170,000.

## Who does not inherit
One who killed the deceased, and a person of a different religion.

## In practice
Estates are divided through the courts. Cases with grandparents, siblings or many heirs become complex, so take the details to a judge or a scholar of inheritance.

- **ފިރިހެން ދަރިއަކު ނެތް އަންހެން ދަރި:** އެކަކުނަމަ ދެބައިކުޅަ އެއްބައި. ދެމީހުން ނުވަތަ އެއަށްވުރެ ގިނަނަމަ ތިންބައިކުޅަ ދެބައި.
- **ފިރިހެން ދަރިންނާއި އަންހެން ދަރިން** ބާކީ ބައި ނަގާނީ، ކޮންމެ ފިރިހެން ދަރިއަކަށް ދެ އަންހެން ދަރިންގެ ބައި ލިބޭ ގޮތަށެވެ (4:11).

ފިރިހެން ދަރިއަށް ބޮޑު ބައެއް ލިބެނީ، ރަން ދިނުމާއި، އަނބިދަރިންނަށް ޚަރަދުކުރުން އޭނާގެ މައްޗަށް ވާޖިބުވާތީއެވެ. އަންހެނާގެ މުދަލަކީ ހަމައެކަނި އޭނާގެ މިލްކެވެ.

## މިސާލެއް
މީހަކު މަރުވިއިރު އަނބިމީހާއާއި، މަންމައާއި، ފިރިހެން ދަރިއަކާއި، އަންހެން ދަރިއަކު ހުއްޓެވެ. ދަރަނި އަދާކުރުމަށްފަހު ތަރިކައަކީ 720,000 ރުފިޔާއެވެ.
- އަނބިމީހާ، އަށްބައިކުޅަ އެއްބައި: 90,000.
- މަންމަ، ހަބައިކުޅަ އެއްބައި: 120,000.
- ބާކީ 510,000 ތިން ބަޔަށް: ފިރިހެން ދަރި 340,000، އަންހެން ދަރި 170,000.

## ވާރުތަ ނުލިބޭ މީހުން
މަރުވި މީހާ ޤަތުލުކުރި މީހާއާއި، އެހެން ދީނެއްގެ މީހާ.

## ޢަމަލީ ގޮތުން
ތަރިކަ ބެހެނީ ކޯޓު މެދުވެރިކޮށެވެ. އުނދަގޫ މައްސަލަތަކުގައި ޤާޟީއެއްގެ ނުވަތަ ޢިލްމުވެރިއެއްގެ ލަފާ ހޯދާށެވެ.

These are matters every Muslim woman needs to know for her worship, and that fathers and husbands should understand. All measures given follow the Shafi'i school.

## Menstruation
- Shortest: a day and a night. Longest: fifteen days. Usual: six or seven.
- The shortest interval of purity between two periods is fifteen days.

During it a woman does not pray, and does **not** make those prayers up. She does not fast, and **does** make the fasts up (Muslim 335). She does not make tawaf, touch the mushaf, recite the Quran as recitation, or stay in the mosque, and marital intercourse is forbidden (2:222). Remembrance, dua, salawat, and listening to the Quran all remain open to her.

When the bleeding stops she makes ghusl and prays from that prayer time onward.

## Bleeding outside the period
Bleeding beyond fifteen days, or less than a day, is **istihadah**, an illness and not menstruation. She washes, protects herself, makes wudu after each prayer time enters, and prays and fasts as normal.

## After childbirth
Post-natal bleeding has no minimum. It usually lasts forty days and at most sixty. Its rulings are those of menstruation.

## Dress and prayer
In prayer she covers everything except the face and hands. Outside the home, before men who are not close relatives, she covers the same with loose, opaque clothing.

## Waiting periods
- After divorce: three periods of purity, or until birth if pregnant (2:228).
- After a husband's death: four months and ten days, or until birth if pregnant (2:234).

## Rights worth knowing
Her property, earnings and inheritance are hers alone. Her consent is required for marriage. She is owed her dowry and maintenance, and seeking knowledge is an obligation on her as on a man.

މިއީ ކޮންމެ މުސްލިމު އަންހެނަކު އޭނާގެ އަޅުކަމަށްޓަކައި ދަންނަންޖެހޭ، އަދި ބައްޕައިންނާއި ފިރިން ދަންނަންވީ ކަންތައްތަކެވެ. މިންތައް ބަޔާންކޮށްފައިވަނީ ޝާފިޢީ މަޛްހަބާ އެއްގޮތަށެވެ.

## ޙައިޟު
- އެންމެ ކުރު: ރެއަކާއި ދުވާލެއް. އެންމެ ދިގު: ފަނަރަ ދުވަސް. ޢާންމު: ހަ ނުވަތަ ހަތް ދުވަސް.
- ދެ ޙައިޟުގެ ދެމެދުގެ އެންމެ ކުރު ޠާހިރުކަމަކީ ފަނަރަ ދުވަހެވެ.

ޙައިޟުގެ ދުވަސްވަރު އަންހެނާ ނަމާދެއް ނުކުރާނެއެވެ. އެ ނަމާދުތައް ޤަޟާއެއް **ނުކުރާނެއެވެ**. ރޯދައެއް ނުހިފާނެއެވެ. އެ ރޯދަތައް ޤަޟާ **ކުރާނެއެވެ** (މުސްލިމް 335). ޠަވާފުކުރުމާއި، މުޞްޙަފުގައި އަތްލުމާއި، ތިލާވަތުގެ ނިޔަތުގައި ޤުރުއާން ކިޔެވުމާއި، މިސްކިތުގައި މަޑުކުރުމާއި، ޖިމާޢުވުން މަނާއެވެ (2:222). ޛިކުރުކުރުމާއި، ދުޢާކުރުމާއި، ޞަލަވާތް ކިޔުމާއި، ޤުރުއާން އަޑުއެހުން ހުއްދައެވެ.

ލޭ ހުއްޓުމުން ހިނައިގަނެ، އެ ނަމާދު ވަގުތުން ފެށިގެން ނަމާދުކުރާނީއެވެ.

## އިސްތިޙާޟާ
ފަނަރަ ދުވަހަށްވުރެ ދިގު، ނުވަތަ ދުވަހަކަށްވުރެ ކުރު ލެޔަކީ **އިސްތިޙާޟާއެވެ**. އެއީ ބައްޔެކެވެ. ދޮވެ، ކޮންމެ ނަމާދު ވަގުތެއް ވަނުމުން ވުޟޫކޮށް، ޢާންމު ގޮތުގައި ނަމާދުކޮށް ރޯދަހިފާނީއެވެ.

## ނިފާސް
ވިހެއުމަށްފަހު އަންނަ ލޭގެ އެންމެ ކުރު މުއްދަތެއް ނެތެވެ. ޢާންމުކޮށް ސާޅީސް ދުވަހެވެ. އެންމެ ދިގުވެގެން ފަސްދޮޅަސް ދުވަހެވެ. ޙުކުމްތަކަކީ ޙައިޟުގެ ޙުކުމްތަކެވެ.

## ހެދުމާއި ނަމާދު
ނަމާދުގައި މޫނާއި ދެއަތްތިލަ ފިޔަވައި މުޅި ހަށިގަނޑު ނިވާކުރާނީއެވެ.

## ޢިއްދަ
- ވަރިއަށްފަހު: ތިން ޠާހިރުކަން، ނުވަތަ މާބަނޑުނަމަ ވިހެއުމާ ހަމައަށް (2:228).
- ފިރިމީހާ މަރުވުމަށްފަހު: ހަތަރު މަހާއި ދިހަ ދުވަސް (2:234).

## ދަންނަންވީ ޙައްޤުތައް
އޭނާގެ މުދަލާއި، އާމްދަނީއާއި، ވާރުތައަކީ ހަމައެކަނި އޭނާގެ މިލްކެވެ. ކައިވެންޏަށް އޭނާގެ ރުހުން ބޭނުންވެއެވެ. ޢިލްމު ހޯދުމަކީ ފިރިހެނާގެ މައްޗަށް ފަދައިން އަންހެނާގެ މައްޗަށްވެސް ވާޖިބެކެވެ.

## Before it begins
Make up any fasts owed from last year. Learn the rules of fasting, in "Fasting in Ramadan". The month starts with the announcement of the new moon by the authorities.

## Each day
- **Before dawn:** eat suhur, even a few dates and water, and make your intention for the day's fast.
- **Fajr to Maghrib:** fast, pray on time, guard the tongue and eyes.
- **Daytime:** read the Quran. One juz' a day completes it in the month, about twenty minutes.
- **Sunset:** break the fast at once with dates or water, make dua, pray Maghrib, then eat.
- **Night:** Isha and **tarawih**. In the Shafi'i school tarawih is twenty rak'ahs prayed in twos, followed by witr; many mosques pray eight. "Whoever stands in Ramadan with faith and seeking reward is forgiven his past sins" (Bukhari 2008).

## Give
The Prophet ﷺ was most generous in Ramadan. Feed someone breaking their fast, and you have the like of their reward.

## The last ten nights
He would stay up the night, wake his family and strive harder (Bukhari 2024).
- Seek **Laylat al-Qadr** on the odd nights. Say: *Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni*.
- **I'tikaf:** staying in the mosque for worship, for the ten days or for whatever time you can.

## Before Eid
Pay **zakat al-fitr** for yourself and each dependant before the Eid prayer. Say the takbir from sunset on the last day until the prayer.

## After
Six fasts in Shawwal after Ramadan are like fasting the whole year (Muslim 1164). Keep one habit from the month alive.

## ފެށުމުގެ ކުރިން
މިދިޔަ އަހަރުގެ ޤަޟާ ރޯދަތައް ހިފާށެވެ. ރޯދައިގެ ޙުކުމްތައް ދަސްކުރާށެވެ. މަސް ފެށެނީ ހަނދު ފެނުނުކަން ވެރިން އިޢުލާންކުރުމުންނެވެ.

## ކޮންމެ ދުވަހަކު
- **ފަތިހުގެ ކުރިން:** ހާރުކައި، އެ ދުވަހުގެ ރޯދައަށް ނިޔަތްގަތުން.
- **ފަތިހުން މަޣްރިބަށް:** ރޯދަހިފައި، ވަގުތަށް ނަމާދުކޮށް، ދުލާއި ލޯ ރައްކާތެރިކުރުން.
- **ދުވާލު:** ޤުރުއާން ކިޔެވުން. ދުވާލަކު އެއް ފޮތް ކިޔެވުމުން މަހުގެ ތެރޭގައި ޚަތިމްކުރެވޭނެއެވެ.
- **އިރުއޮއްސުމުން:** ކަދުރުން ނުވަތަ ފެނުން އަވަހަށް ރޯދަ ވީއްލައި، ދުޢާކޮށް، މަޣްރިބު ނަމާދުކުރުން.
- **ރޭގަނޑު:** ޢިޝާއާއި **ތަރާވީޙު**. ޝާފިޢީ މަޛްހަބުގައި ތަރާވީޙަކީ ވިހި ރަކުޢަތެވެ. ގިނަ މިސްކިތްތަކުގައި އަށް ރަކުޢަތް ކުރެއެވެ. "އީމާންކަމާއެކު ސަވާބަށް އެދި ރަމަޟާން މަހު ރޭއަޅުކަންކުރާ މީހާގެ ކުރީގެ ފާފަތައް ފުއްސެވޭނެއެވެ" (ބުޚާރީ 2008).

## ޞަދަޤާތް
ނަބިއްޔާ ﷺ އެންމެ ދީލަތިވެވަޑައިގަންނަވަނީ ރަމަޟާން މަހުއެވެ. ރޯދަވެރިއަކަށް ރޯދަ ވީއްލަން ދޭށެވެ.

## ފަހު ދިހަ ރޭ
- އޮނަހިރި ރޭތަކުގައި **ލައިލަތުލް ޤަދްރު** ހޯދާށެވެ. *ﷲުއްމަ އިންނަކަ ޢަފުއްވުން ތުޙިއްބުލް ޢަފްވަ ފަޢުފު ޢަންނީ* ކިޔާށެވެ.
- **އިޢުތިކާފު:** އަޅުކަމަށްޓަކައި މިސްކިތުގައި މަޑުކުރުން.

## ޢީދުގެ ކުރިން
ޢީދު ނަމާދުގެ ކުރިން އަމިއްލަ ނަފްސާއި ބެލުމުގެ ދަށުގައިވާ ކޮންމެ މީހަކަށް **ފިޠުރު ޒަކާތް** ދައްކާށެވެ.

## އޭގެ ފަހުން

@@ eid-udhiyah
title_en: The Eid prayer and the sacrifice
title_dv: ޢީދު ނަމާދާއި އުޟްޙިޔާ
ref: Quran 108:2; 22:36-37; Bukhari 956, 5545
--- en
Islam has two festivals: **Eid al-Fitr** on 1 Shawwal, after Ramadan, and **Eid al-Adha** on 10 Dhul-Hijjah, in the days of hajj.

## Sunnahs of the day
- Bathe, wear your best clothes, use perfume.
- On Eid al-Fitr eat an odd number of dates before leaving; on Eid al-Adha eat after the prayer.
- Say the takbir aloud on the way: *Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar, Allahu akbar, wa lillahil-hamd.*
- Go by one route and return by another.
- Greet others: *Taqabbalallahu minna wa minkum* (May Allah accept from us and from you).

## The Eid prayer
Two rak'ahs after sunrise, with no adhan or iqamah, followed by a sermon. In the Shafi'i school:
1. Opening takbir and opening dua, then **seven** extra takbirs, raising the hands each time.
2. Al-Fatihah and a surah aloud, then complete the rak'ah.
3. In the second rak'ah, **five** extra takbirs after standing, then recite and complete the prayer.

It is a strongly emphasised sunnah, and women and children attend.

## The sacrifice (udhiyah)
An emphasised sunnah for whoever can afford it, in memory of Ibrahim.
- **When:** after the Eid al-Adha prayer until sunset on 13 Dhul-Hijjah.
- **What:** a sheep or goat for one household; a cow or camel shared by up to seven. A sheep must be a year old, a goat two, a cow two, a camel five, and free of obvious defects.
- **How:** say *Bismillah, Allahu akbar* and slaughter with a sharp knife, kindly.
- **The meat:** eat some, give some as gifts and give some to the poor. The butcher is not paid from it.

One who intends to sacrifice leaves hair and nails uncut from the start of Dhul-Hijjah (Muslim 1977). "Their meat and blood do not reach Allah; what reaches Him is your mindfulness" (22:37).

Fasting is forbidden on the two Eid days and the three days after Eid al-Adha.

## ޢީދު ދުވަހުގެ ސުންނަތްތައް
- ހިނައިގަނެ، އެންމެ ރީތި ހެދުން ލައި، ސެންޓު ލުން.
- ފިޠުރު ޢީދުގައި ނުކުތުމުގެ ކުރިން އޮނަހިރި ޢަދަދަކަށް ކަދުރު ކެއުން. އަޟްޙާ ޢީދުގައި ނަމާދަށްފަހު ކެއުން.
- މަގުމަތީގައި އަޑުހަރުކޮށް ތަކްބީރު ކިޔުން: *ﷲު އަކްބަރު، ﷲު އަކްބަރު، ﷲު އަކްބަރު، ލާ އިލާހަ އިއްލަﷲު، ވަﷲު އަކްބަރު، ﷲު އަކްބަރު، ވަލިﷲިލް ޙަމްދު.*
- އެއް މަގަކުން ގޮސް އެހެން މަގަކުން އައުން.
- *ތަޤައްބަލަﷲު މިންނާ ވަމިންކުމް* ކިޔައި ތަހުނިޔާ ކިޔުން.

## ޢީދު ނަމާދު
އިރުއެރުމަށްފަހު، ބަންގިއާއި ޤަމަތަކާ ނުލައި ކުރެވޭ ދެ ރަކުޢަތެވެ. އެއަށްފަހު ޚުޠުބާއެވެ. ޝާފިޢީ މަޛްހަބުގައި:
1. ތަކްބީރަތުލް އިޙްރާމާއި ދުޢާއުލް އިސްތިފްތާޙަށްފަހު އިތުރު **ހަތް** ތަކްބީރު.
2. ފާތިޙާއާއި ސޫރަތެއް އަޑުހަރުކޮށް ކިޔަވައި ރަކުޢަތް ފުރިހަމަކުރުން.
3. ދެވަނަ ރަކުޢަތުގައި އިތުރު **ފަސް** ތަކްބީރު.

މިއީ ސުންނަތް މުއައްކަދާއެކެވެ. އަންހެނުންނާއި ކުޑަކުދިންވެސް ޙާޟިރުވާނެއެވެ.

## އުޟްޙިޔާ
ތަނަވަސްކަން ހުރި މީހާއަށް، އިބްރާހީމްގެފާނުގެ ހަނދާނުގައި ކުރެވޭ ސުންނަތް މުއައްކަދާއެކެވެ.
- **ވަގުތު:** އަޟްޙާ ޢީދު ނަމާދަށްފަހު ޛުލްޙިއްޖާ 13 ގެ އިރުއޮއްސުމާ ހަމައަށް.
- **ޖަނަވާރު:** އެއް ގެއަކަށް ކަންބަޅިއެއް ނުވަތަ ބަކަރިއެއް. ހަތް މީހުންނަށް ގެރިއެއް ނުވަތަ ޖަމަލެއް.
- **ގޮތް:** *ބިސްމިﷲި، ﷲު އަކްބަރު* ކިޔައި ތޫނު ވަޅިއަކުން ކަތިލުން.
- **މަސް:** ބައެއް ކައި، ބައެއް ހަދިޔާކޮށް، ބައެއް ފަޤީރުންނަށް ދިނުން.

"އެ ތަކެތީގެ މަހެއް، ލެޔެއް ﷲ ގެ ޙަޟްރަތަކަށް ނުފޯރާނެއެވެ. އެކަލާނގެ ޙަޟްރަތަށް ފޯރާނީ ތިޔަބައިމީހުންގެ ތަޤްވާވެރިކަމެވެ" (22:37).

ދެ ޢީދު ދުވަހާއި އަޟްޙާ ޢީދަށްފަހު ތިން ދުވަހު ރޯދަހިފުން ޙަރާމެވެ.

**Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari** (194-256 AH / 810-870 CE) was born in Bukhara, in today's Uzbekistan. His father died when he was small, and his mother raised him. He had memorised whole books of hadith before he was sixteen, when he went on hajj with her and stayed in the Hijaz to study.

For decades he travelled between Makkah, Madinah, Basra, Kufa, Baghdad, Egypt and Syria, hearing hadith from more than a thousand teachers. His memory was tested famously in Baghdad, where scholars mixed up the chains and texts of a hundred hadith; he corrected every one.

His great work, known as **Sahih al-Bukhari**, took sixteen years. He selected its hadith from some six hundred thousand reports, accepting only narrators proven to have met one another, and is said to have prayed two rak'ahs before recording each one. Muslims regard it as the most authentic book after the Quran. The chapter headings show his own understanding of law.

He also wrote *al-Adab al-Mufrad* on manners and *at-Tarikh al-Kabir* on narrators. His students included Muslim and at-Tirmidhi.

Late in life he was forced out of Bukhara after refusing to give the governor's sons private lessons, saying knowledge is sought, not delivered. He died in a village near Samarqand.

**Yahya ibn Sharaf an-Nawawi** (631-676 AH / 1233-1277 CE) was born in Nawa, a village south of Damascus. As a boy he preferred reciting the Quran to playing, and a passing scholar told his father the child would become the most learned and devout man of his age.

At eighteen he entered a school in Damascus. He attended twelve lessons a day, slept little and lived on bread his father sent from the village. Within a few years he was teaching, and he later headed the Dar al-Hadith al-Ashrafiyyah without taking its salary.

He died at forty-five, unmarried, and in about twenty years of writing produced books that Muslims of every school still use daily:

- **The Forty Hadith**, in this app's Hadith section.
- **Riyad as-Salihin**, hadith on character and worship.
- **Al-Adhkar**, the supplications of the day and night.
- **Sharh Sahih Muslim**, the standard commentary on that collection.
- **Minhaj at-Talibin** and **al-Majmu'**, the reference works of the Shafi'i school, the school followed in the Maldives.

He was known for speaking plainly to rulers. When the Sultan Baybars demanded a fatwa allowing a tax on the people for war, an-Nawawi refused while the sultan's own treasury was full, and was sent away from Damascus for it.

- **ރިޔާޟުއްޞާލިޙީން**.
- **އަލްއަޛްކާރު**.
- **ޝަރްޙު ޞަޙީޙި މުސްލިމް**.

**Abu Hamid Muhammad al-Ghazali** (450-505 AH / 1058-1111 CE) was born in Tus, in north-eastern Iran, to a poor wool-spinner who died young and left his sons to a friend to educate. He studied in Nishapur under al-Juwayni, the leading Shafi'i and Ash'ari scholar of the day.

At thirty-four he was appointed head of the **Nizamiyyah** college in Baghdad, the most prestigious teaching post in the Muslim world. Hundreds attended his lectures. He wrote on law, legal theory and theology, and a critique of the philosophers, *Tahafut al-Falasifah*.

Four years later he fell into a crisis. He found that he was teaching for fame, and for a time he could not speak or eat. In 488 AH he left Baghdad, gave away his wealth and spent about ten years in seclusion in Damascus, Jerusalem, Makkah and Madinah.

Out of those years came **Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Din**, "The Revival of the Religious Sciences", in forty books: worship, daily conduct, the vices that destroy and the virtues that save. Its aim is to join outward practice to inward sincerity. He told his own story in *al-Munqidh min ad-Dalal*.

He returned to teach briefly, then went home to Tus, where he died. Later scholars corrected weak hadith in the *Ihya'*, and its place as a guide to sincerity has remained.

**Ahmad ibn 'Ali ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani** (773-852 AH / 1372-1449 CE) was born in Cairo and orphaned as a small child. A guardian raised him. He memorised the Quran at nine and led tarawih in Makkah at twelve.

He became the foremost hadith scholar of his time and was given the title *Amir al-Mu'minin fil-Hadith*, "commander of the faithful in hadith". His works number well over a hundred:

- **Fath al-Bari**, a commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari in many volumes, written over about twenty-five years. Its completion was celebrated with a public feast in Cairo. A later scholar, asked to write a commentary on al-Bukhari, replied that there was no need after it.

- **Tahdhib at-Tahdhib** and **Taqrib at-Tahdhib**, on the reliability of narrators.
- **Al-Isabah**, biographies of the Companions.
- **Nukhbat al-Fikar**, a short primer on the science of hadith.

## Arrival
In 711 CE **Tariq ibn Ziyad** crossed from North Africa with a small army; the rock where he landed still carries his name, Jabal Tariq, Gibraltar. Within a few years most of the Iberian peninsula was under Muslim rule and was called **al-Andalus**.

## Cordoba
In 756 **'Abd ar-Rahman I**, an Umayyad prince who had escaped the fall of his family in Damascus, founded an independent emirate. In 929 his descendant 'Abd ar-Rahman III declared a caliphate. Tenth-century **Cordoba** had paved and lit streets, hundreds of mosques and baths, and a library said to hold four hundred thousand volumes. Muslims, Christians and Jews lived and worked side by side, and scholars from across Europe came to study.

## Its scholars
- **Az-Zahrawi**, the surgeon whose illustrated manual was used in Europe for centuries.
- **Ibn Rushd** (Averroes), judge, physician and philosopher.
- **Al-Qurtubi**, author of one of the great tafsirs.
- **Ibn Hazm**, jurist and writer.
- **Ibn 'Abd al-Barr**, hadith scholar.

## Decline
In 1031 the caliphate broke into small rival kingdoms that fought one another and paid tribute to the Christian north. Toledo fell in 1085, Cordoba in 1236, Seville in 1248. Only **Granada** remained, where the Alhambra palace was built. It surrendered in 1492. The Muslims who stayed were later forced to convert or leave, and the last were expelled in 1609-1614.

## What it left, and what it teaches
Words, crops, irrigation, architecture, and the transmission of science and philosophy to Europe. Its historians drew one lesson above the others: it flourished when united and just, and was lost through division.

756 ގައި ދިމިޝްޤުން ސަލާމަތްވެވަޑައިގަތް އުމަވީ ޝަހްޒާދާ **ޢަބްދުއްރަޙްމާން 1** މިނިވަން އިމާރާތެއް ޤާއިމުކުރެއްވިއެވެ. 929 ގައި ޢަބްދުއްރަޙްމާން 3 ޚިލާފަތު އިޢުލާންކުރެއްވިއެވެ. ދިހަވަނަ ޤަރުނުގެ **ޤުރްޠުބާގައި** އަލިކޮށްފައިވާ މަގުތަކާއި، ސަތޭކަގަނޑު މިސްކިތާއި، ހަތަރު ލައްކަ ފޮތް ހުރި ކަމަށް ބުނެވޭ ލައިބްރަރީއެއް ހުއްޓެވެ. މުސްލިމުންނާއި، ނަޞާރާއިންނާއި، ޔަހޫދީން އެކުގައި ދިރިއުޅުނެވެ.

## ޢިލްމުވެރިން
- **އައްޒަހްރާވީ**، ޖަރާޙީ ޑޮކްޓަރު.
- **އިބްނު ރުޝްދު**، ޤާޟީ، ޑޮކްޓަރު އަދި ފަލްސަފާވެރިޔާ.
- **އަލްޤުރްޠުބީ**، މަޝްހޫރު ތަފްސީރުގެ މުއައްލިފު.
- **އިބްނު ޙަޒްމު** އަދި **އިބްނު ޢަބްދިލް ބައްރު**.

## ވެއްޓުން
1031 ގައި ޚިލާފަތު ކުދިކުދި ރަސްކަންތަކަކަށް ބައިބައިވެ، އެކަކު އަނެކަކާ ހަނގުރާމަކުރިއެވެ. 1085 ގައި ޠުލައިޠުލާ، 1236 ގައި ޤުރްޠުބާ، 1248 ގައި އިޝްބީލިއްޔާ ވެއްޓުނެވެ. ބާކީ އޮތީ ހަމައެކަނި **ޣަރްނާޠާ** އެވެ. 1492 ގައި އެ ރަށްވެސް ޙަވާލުކުރެވުނެވެ. ފަހުން މުސްލިމުން ދީން ބަދަލުކުރުމަށް ނުވަތަ ޤައުމު ދޫކުރުމަށް މަޖުބޫރުކުރެވުނެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތް
އެއްބައިވަންތަކަމާއި ޢަދުލުވެރިކަން އޮތްއިރު ކުރިއެރިއެވެ. ބައިބައިވުމުގެ ސަބަބުން ގެއްލުނެވެ.

## The First Crusade
In 1099 an army from western Europe captured Jerusalem and killed most of its Muslim and Jewish inhabitants. Al-Aqsa became a palace and stable. The Muslim lands around it were divided among rival rulers, which is how a distant army had succeeded.

## Unity first
The recovery began with 'Imad ad-Din Zangi and his son **Nur ad-Din**, who united Syria, founded schools and hospitals, and had a pulpit built for al-Aqsa decades before it was freed.

**Salah ad-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub** (1137-1193), a Kurdish officer in Nur ad-Din's service, became ruler of Egypt and then, over twelve patient years, brought Egypt and Syria together. He lived simply, attended hadith lessons and gave away what came to him.

## Hattin and Jerusalem
In July 1187 he destroyed the Crusader army at **Hattin**. On 2 October 1187, 27 Rajab 583 AH, Jerusalem surrendered. There was no massacre. The inhabitants were ransomed for a small sum; he and his brother paid for thousands of the poor themselves, and escorts guarded those who left. Churches stayed open, and Jews were allowed to return. Nur ad-Din's pulpit was installed in al-Aqsa.

## The Third Crusade
Richard of England arrived and fought him for three years without retaking the city. In 1192 they agreed a truce that kept Jerusalem under Muslim rule and open to Christian pilgrims.

He died in Damascus a few months later. The treasury held too little to pay for his funeral.

## What it teaches
Reform and unity came before victory, and mercy in victory is remembered longer than the victory itself.

## ފުރަތަމަ ޞަލީބީ ހަނގުރާމަ
1099 ގައި ހުޅަނގު ޔޫރަޕުގެ ލަޝްކަރަކުން ޤުދުސް ހިފައި، އެތަނުގެ މުސްލިމުންނާއި ޔަހޫދީންގެ ގިނަ ބަޔަކު ޤަތުލުކުރިއެވެ. ވަށައިގެންވާ މުސްލިމު ބިންތައް އޮތީ ވާދަވެރި ވެރިންގެ ދެމެދުގައި ބައިބައިވެފައެވެ.

## ފުރަތަމަ އެއްބައިވަންތަކަން
އަނބުރާ ހޯދުމުގެ މަސައްކަތް ފެށުނީ ޢިމާދުއްދީން ޒަންގީއާއި ދަރިކަލުން **ނޫރުއްދީނުގެ** އަތްޕުޅުންނެވެ. ނޫރުއްދީން ޝާމު އެއްބައިކުރައްވައި، މަދަރުސާތަކާއި ހޮސްޕިޓަލުތައް ބިނާކުރައްވައި، އަޤްޞާ މިސްކިތަށް މިންބަރެއް ތައްޔާރުކުރެއްވިއެވެ.

**ޞަލާޙުއްދީން ޔޫސުފު ބިން އައްޔޫބު** (1137-1193) މިޞްރުގެ ވެރިކަމާ ޙަވާލުވެ، ބާރަ އަހަރުގެ ތެރޭގައި މިޞްރާއި ޝާމު އެއްބައިކުރެއްވިއެވެ. ސާދާ ދިރިއުޅުމެއް އުޅުއްވައި، ލިބުނު ހުރިހާ އެއްޗެއް ޞަދަޤާތްކުރެއްވިއެވެ.

## ޙިއްޠީނާއި ޤުދުސް
1187 ގެ ޖުލައި މަހު **ޙިއްޠީނުގައި** ޞަލީބީ ލަޝްކަރު ބަލިކުރެއްވިއެވެ. 1187 އޮކްޓޫބަރު 2، ހިޖުރައިން 583 ގެ ރަޖަބު 27 ގައި ޤުދުސް ޙަވާލުކުރެވުނެވެ. ޤަތުލުޢާންމެއް ނުހިންގެވިއެވެ. ރަށުގެ މީހުންނަށް ކުޑަ ފިދާއެއްގެ ބަދަލުގައި ދިއުމުގެ ހުއްދަ ދެއްވިއެވެ. ހާސްގަނޑު ފަޤީރުންގެ ފިދާ އެކަލޭގެފާނާއި ބޭބޭފުޅު ދެއްކެވިއެވެ. ފައްޅިތައް ހުޅުވިފައި ހުއްޓެވެ.

## ތިންވަނަ ޞަލީބީ ހަނގުރާމަ
އިނގިރޭސި ރަސްގެފާނު ރިޗަޑް ތިން އަހަރު ހަނގުރާމަކުރިނަމަވެސް ޤުދުސް ނުހިފުނެވެ. 1192 ގައި ޞުލްޙައެއް ވެވުނެވެ.

މަދު މަހެއްފަހުން ދިމިޝްޤުގައި އަވަހާރަވިއެވެ. ޖަނާޒާގެ ޚަރަދަށް ފުދޭ ފައިސާ ޚަޒާނާގައި ނެތެވެ.

## ޢިބްރަތް
ނަޞްރުގެ ކުރިން އައީ އިޞްލާޙާއި އެއްބައިވަންތަކަމެވެ. ނަޞްރުގައި ދައްކާ ރަޙްމަތް، ނަޞްރަށްވުރެ ދިގު މުއްދަތަކަށް ހަނދާންކުރެވެއެވެ.
